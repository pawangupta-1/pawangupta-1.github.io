I'm an engineer turned education researcher. Before graduate study, I worked across India managing a technology-driven learning program spanning 40+ partner organizations in five states, and during the COVID-19 disruption I helped launch tools to support remote teaching. Since 2023 I've been a Research Associate in the Department of Curriculum, Instruction, and Special Education (CISE) at the University of Virginia's School of Education and Human Development, where my work sits at the intersection of learning sciences, human-computer interaction, and data science.

I've presented and published research on AI-powered learning analytics dashboards at venues including AERA and LAK, and I serve as an EdTech mentor with the International Centre for EdTech Impact, advising on learning architecture, product strategy, and evidence design. I hold a Master's degree and am currently preparing PhD applications to continue this line of work.

Outside the research, I build things — Project Apnapan, an effort to make schools feel like places every child belongs, is the current one. I also write poetry, which is less a hobby than another way of asking the same questions I ask in my research: what helps a person grow, and what gets in the way.

What I care about

Most technology asks people to adapt to it. I'm interested in the opposite: technology that adapts to people, enriching the environments and processes around them so their own capacity for learning, agency, and wellbeing can do the rest. That belief runs through the research, the products I build, and — quietly — the poems too.
